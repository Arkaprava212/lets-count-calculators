What this calculator does

Pace is the time you take to cover one kilometre or one mile. Speed is the distance you cover in one hour. Runners and walkers usually talk about pace, and cyclists usually talk about speed, so this page shows both. It also gives a table of split times so you can see where you should be at each kilometre or mile if you hold an even pace.

How it is calculated

Pace = total time ÷ distance
Speed = distance ÷ total time (in hours)
Finish time = pace × distance
1 mile = 1.609344 km

The calculator turns your time into seconds, divides by the distance, and shows the answer as minutes and seconds. For the other unit it multiplies or divides by 1.609344. Split times assume you never speed up or slow down.

Worked example

Someone completes a 10 km run in 55 minutes. Total time is 55 × 60 = 3,300 seconds. Pace is 3,300 ÷ 10 = 330 seconds per km, which is 5:30 per km. Speed is 10 ÷ (55 ÷ 60) = 10.9 km/h. In miles, 330 × 1.609344 ≈ 531 seconds, so the pace is about 8:51 per mile. At an even pace, they would pass 5 km after 27:30. Limitations

  • The calculator assumes one steady pace. Real runs have hills, stops, traffic lights and tired legs, so your own kilometres will vary.
  • Your result is only as accurate as the time and distance you enter. GPS distance can differ slightly from a measured route.
  • Entries outside a realistic range (pace faster than 1:00 or slower than 20:00 per km) are rejected as probable typing mistakes.
  • It describes your effort for one session. It does not say whether a pace is right for your health or fitness.

Common questions

How do I calculate my running pace?

Divide your total time by your distance. For example, 55 minutes for 10 km is 55 ÷ 10 = 5.5 minutes per km, which is 5:30 per km.

How do I change minutes per km into minutes per mile?

Multiply the pace in seconds per km by 1.609344. A pace of 5:30 per km is 330 seconds, and 330 × 1.609344 ≈ 531 seconds, which is about 8:51 per mile.

What is a good pace for a beginner?

There is no single good pace. Many beginners run somewhere between about 7 and 10 minutes per km, but age, fitness and weather all matter. A useful starting point is a pace where you can still speak in full sentences.

Why does my watch or app show a different average pace?

Many apps show moving pace, which leaves out time when you stopped, while this calculator uses the total time you enter. GPS distance can also differ slightly from the official race distance.
