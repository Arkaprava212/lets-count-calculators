What this calculator does

Training zones are ranges of heart rate that match different levels of effort. Easy zones build endurance and help recovery, and hard zones are used in short, planned doses. Without a lab test, the usual way to set zones is to estimate your maximum heart rate from your age and take percentages of it. This calculator does that for you.

How it is calculated

Maximum heart rate (Tanaka) = 208 − 0.7 × age
Maximum heart rate (classic) = 220 − age
Without resting heart rate: zone = % × maximum
With resting heart rate: zone = resting + % × (maximum − resting)

The five zones use 50–60%, 60–70%, 70–80%, 80–90% and 90–100%. The second method with a resting heart rate is called the Karvonen method, or heart rate reserve. It usually gives higher zones for fit people with a low resting heart rate.

Worked example

A 30-year-old uses the Tanaka formula: 208 − 0.7 × 30 = 187 beats per minute. With no resting heart rate, Zone 2 (60–70%) is 0.6 × 187 ≈ 112 to 0.7 × 187 ≈ 131 beats per minute. If their resting heart rate is 60, the reserve is 187 − 60 = 127, and Zone 2 becomes 60 + 0.6 × 127 ≈ 136 to 60 + 0.7 × 127 ≈ 149. Press "Try an example" above to see all five zones.

Limitations

  • A formula is an average. Your real maximum can be 10 beats per minute or more higher or lower.
  • Heart rate changes with heat, stress, caffeine, sleep, illness, altitude and some medicines.
  • The zones are a guide for effort, not a prescription. How you feel and whether you can talk are also useful checks.
  • The calculator accepts ages 10 to 100 and resting heart rates between 30 and 120 beats per minute, and rejects values outside these ranges.
  • It is not medical advice. If you have a heart or health condition, or feel chest pain or dizziness during exercise, stop and see a doctor.

Common questions

How do I find my maximum heart rate?

The only accurate ways are a supervised stress test or a very hard, safe effort measured by a heart rate monitor. For most people, a formula based on age is a reasonable starting estimate, but your own maximum can differ by 10 beats per minute or more.

Which formula should I choose, Tanaka or 220 minus age?

Both are averages from groups of people. The Tanaka formula (208 − 0.7 × age) was built from a larger set of studies and tends to fit adults over 40 better than 220 − age, so it is the default here.

Which zone is best for beginners?

Most of a beginner's sessions are usually done at an easy effort, around Zone 2, where you can speak in full sentences. Harder zones are used sparingly. If you are unsure, start easy and build up.

Do medicines or health conditions change these zones?

Yes. Some medicines, such as beta-blockers, lower heart rate, and some heart conditions need special limits. If you take regular medicine or have a heart, blood pressure or other health condition, ask your doctor which heart rate range is safe for you.
