What this calculator does

Doing too much too soon is a common reason people stop training. This planner shows how many weeks it takes to move from your current weekly distance to a higher one if you add a modest amount each week and take an easier week now and then. It works for running and walking, and gives a rough guide for cycling.

How it is calculated

Build week = previous build week × (1 + increase), never above your goal
Every 4th week = 80% of the last build week (recovery)
Long run ≈ 30% of the week's total

Week 1 is your current distance. Each following build week adds up to the percentage you choose (10% or 5%) to the previous build week. Every fourth week is a recovery week. After a recovery week, the next build week continues from the previous build week, not from the lighter one. Once you reach the goal, later weeks hold at that level.

Worked example

Someone covers 20 km a week and wants 30 km a week within 10 weeks, adding up to 10% each week. The plan is 20, 22, 24.2, then a recovery week at 19.4 (80% of 24.2), then 26.6, 29.3 and 30 km in week 7. Week 8 is another recovery week at 24 km, and weeks 9 and 10 hold at 30 km. The long run would be about 9 km in a 30 km week. Press "Try an example" above to see the full table.

Limitations

  • Adding about 10% a week is a popular rule of thumb, not a proven safe limit. Many people, especially beginners, need to build more slowly.
  • The planner only counts distance. It does not know your injuries, health, sleep, age, terrain or how hard your sessions are.
  • It does not schedule individual runs, rest days, strength work or a race taper.
  • If the goal is not reachable in your chosen number of weeks, the result says how far you get and how many weeks you would need.
  • It is not medical or coaching advice. Stop and see a doctor if you feel pain, dizziness or chest discomfort.

Common questions

What is the 10% rule?

It is a popular rule of thumb that suggests increasing your weekly distance by no more than about 10% from one week to the next. It is a guide, not a proven safe limit. Some people need slower increases, especially beginners and anyone returning from a break.

Why does the plan include recovery weeks?

Many coaches schedule a lighter week every third or fourth week so the body can adapt to the extra work. This planner uses every fourth week at 80% of the last build week.

Can I use it for cycling?

You can enter cycling distance, but cyclists often plan by hours rather than distance because speed changes so much with terrain and wind. Use it as a rough guide only.

Is this plan safe if I am coming back from an injury or a health problem?

This planner cannot judge that. It only does arithmetic on the numbers you enter. If you are injured, ill, pregnant or have a health condition, ask a doctor or a qualified coach before increasing your training.
