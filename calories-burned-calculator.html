What this calculator does

It estimates how much energy you used during one run, walk or ride. Energy is shown in kilocalories (kcal), which most people simply call calories. The estimate depends on your body weight, how long you moved, and how fast you went.

How it is calculated

Calories = MET × body weight (kg) × time (hours)
Average speed = distance ÷ time

MET stands for metabolic equivalent. One MET is the energy you use sitting still, and a harder activity has a higher number. Your average speed decides which MET value is used, taken from the Compendium of Physical Activities, a public research table. Values between published speeds are interpolated for running and walking. If you leave the distance blank, a typical value is used: 9.8 for running, 3.5 for walking and 8.0 for cycling.

Worked example

A person weighing 70 kg runs 5 km in 30 minutes. Average speed is 5 ÷ 0.5 = 10 km/h, which gives a MET value of about 10.0. Time is 0.5 hours. Calories = 10.0 × 70 × 0.5 ≈ 350 kcal. The page shows this as a rounded estimate with an illustrative range. Press "Try an example" above to see it.

Limitations

  • This is an estimate for an average person. Fitness, age, sex, body composition, terrain, wind and running style all change the real value.
  • The figure includes the energy your body would use at rest during that time, so it is higher than the extra calories caused by the exercise alone.
  • The range shown is for illustration. It is not a measured error margin.
  • Distance and time that give a speed outside the usual range for the activity (for example running over 25 km/h) are rejected as probable typing mistakes.
  • It is not medical or diet advice.

Common questions

How many calories do I burn running 5 km?

A common rule of thumb is about 1 kcal per kg of body weight for each kilometre run, so a 70 kg person burns roughly 350 kcal over 5 km. The calculator gives a similar answer and adjusts for your speed.

Why does my watch show a different number?

Watches use their own formulas, often based on heart rate, age and sex. Two tools can differ by 20% or more for the same workout, and none of them is exact.

Does walking burn fewer calories than running?

Per hour, yes. Per kilometre the gap is smaller, because walking the same distance takes longer. The calculator shows both if you try the same distance with each activity.

Should I eat back the calories this shows?

This tool cannot tell you how much to eat. It only gives a rough estimate of energy used during one session. For weight or diet goals, speak to a doctor or a registered dietitian.
